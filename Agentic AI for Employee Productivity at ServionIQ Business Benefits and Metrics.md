# Agentic AI for Employee Productivity at ServionIQ: Business Benefits and Metrics

# An agentic assistant that triages mail, schedules meetings and tracks follow-ups, with a person approving every action, gives ServionIQ back employee time and tightens responsiveness, and six metrics show whether it does.

## Business benefits

The main benefit is recovered time on coordination work, with the agent doing the drafting and searching and the employee making the decisions.

| Benefit | How the agentic assistant delivers it | Who gains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Less time on inbox and admin | Triages mail into urgent, needs-reply and FYI, and saves reply drafts for approval instead of starting from a blank page | Every employee, especially managers and client-facing staff |
| Faster scheduling | Finds free slots, checks every invitee's free/busy, and books only after approval, replacing the email back-and-forth | Employees, hiring and project coordinators |
| Fewer dropped commitments | Scans sent mail, meetings and chat for promises and unanswered asks, then proposes a recap, task or reminder | Team leads, account owners |
| Better meeting preparation | Weekday morning briefs combine calendar, mail, chat and documents, and a Friday review shows what slipped | Managers, senior staff |
| Quicker response to colleagues and clients | Event triggers triage new mail and flag meeting requests as they arrive, not at the next inbox check | Customer-facing teams |
| Risk-controlled automation | Agents only propose; a person approves each action, and every proposal is stored with its status | Compliance, IT and security owners |
| Consistency across the organization | One rule set for working hours, weekday-only meetings and external-recipient flags applies to everyone | Operations, leadership |

The approval step is also a business benefit: it lets ServionIQ adopt automation without handing sent mail or calendar changes to an unsupervised system.

## Metrics to track

Six metrics cover time saved, speed, reliability and trust; the first three are the core productivity measures and the last three guard against the agent doing harm.

| Metric | Definition and formula |
| --- | --- |
| 1\. Time spent on email and admin per employee | Weekly minutes on inbox handling, meeting logistics and follow-ups, from a short employee time survey plus calendar and mail activity, compared with the pre-launch baseline |
| 2\. Time to schedule a meeting | Hours from the first meeting request to a confirmed calendar event |
| 3\. Email response time | Median hours from receiving a thread to the user's reply, for threads that need a reply |
| 4\. Follow-up completion rate | Share of detected commitments and open loops closed within the agreed window (for example 3 days) |
| 5\. Proposal approval rate | Proposals approved and run, divided by proposals made, per agent |
| 6\. Failed or refused executions | Execution attempts that returned a failure (slot taken, invitee busy, draft missing) divided by attempts |

**How the metrics relate**

- Metrics 1 to 3 show the business gain: less time spent and faster turnaround.
- Metric 4 shows whether the agent improves delivery on commitments, not just speed.
- Metric 5 measures trust and draft quality. A low rate means proposals are poor; a rate near 100% may mean people approve without reading, so review a sample of approved items.
- Metric 6 measures reliability, so an increase points to a fault or a calendar-sharing gap that needs fixing.

Targets are not set here because ServionIQ's current figures are not known; set each one against the baseline described below.

## Measurement approach

Measure a baseline before launch, then compare a pilot group with a similar group that does not use the assistant.

1. **Baseline:** collect metrics 1 to 4 for about four weeks before launch, so targets reflect ServionIQ's own figures.
2. **Pilot:** start with one or two teams, and review the metrics every two weeks.
3. **Compare:** judge gains against the non-pilot group, so seasonal workload or other changes are not credited to the assistant.
4. **Sample for quality:** review a sample of approved drafts and bookings each month for tone, accuracy and wrong recipients.
5. **Set targets:** agree a target for each metric with team leads once the baseline is known.
